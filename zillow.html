01 — The problem

Renters can find plenty of listings. The hard part is knowing what is complete, current, and actually worth pursuing.

Renter feedback revealed three connected problems: information is fragmented across apps, availability can disagree with a property’s own website, and after browsing enough “good” options, choosing between them becomes exhausting.

Zillow↔ Apartments.com↔ Google Maps↔ Google / Yelp↔ Xiaohongshu↔ Property website
Users cross-check multiple sources because no single surface feels complete — and the same floor plan may appear available in one place but unavailable on the property’s own site.
The opportunity is not to show renters more homes. It is to give them one clearer, more current view of the few homes they might actually choose.
02 — Research

What renters said was breaking the search experience.

Informal renter conversations consistently pointed to the same pattern: people do not trust one app to tell the whole story, so they assemble the decision themselves across several sources.

FINDING 01
No single rental app feels complete.
Implication: Bring pricing, commute, reviews, floor-plan details, and application context into one decision surface.
FINDING 02
Marketplace availability can disagree with the property website.
Implication: Show when unit inventory was last verified and distinguish live, stale, and unavailable floor plans.
FINDING 03
After enough browsing, several options start to look equally good.
Implication: Shift from discovery to personalized comparison before decision fatigue sets in.
FINDING 04
Commute, facilities, parking, price, and reviews shape the final choice.
Implication: Let renters choose which attributes deserve space in the comparison table.
Observed behavior: renters check multiple rental apps, then validate promising properties with maps, review platforms, social content, and the property’s own website before deciding.
MANAGED APARTMENTS

Different friction after search

Income requirements, application fees, external leasing portals, and property-specific websites add learning cost. Availability can also change between the marketplace and the official leasing site.

PRIVATE RENTALS

Different kinds of uncertainty

Landlord reliability, maintenance expectations, utilities, neighborhood reality, noise, parking, and lease flexibility become more important than polished amenities.

A decision workspace, not just a listing browser.

Zillow Focus combines stronger information coverage with availability confidence, then helps renters narrow and compare a smaller set of homes around their own priorities.

Reduce context switching at the moment of decision.

  • Third-party ratings with source transparency
  • Income requirements

04 — User feedback

Three things changed the direction.

The goal became less about adding features — and more about making rental decisions easier to trust.

01

Information is fragmented.

Renters check several rental apps because no single platform feels complete.

Design response Bring commute, reviews, costs, and application details closer to the listing.
02

Availability is hard to trust.

Marketplace listings and official property websites can show different floor plans or availability.

Design response Show when each floor plan was last verified and whether inventory needs a refresh.
03

Too many good options become noise.

After enough browsing, several homes can all look “good enough,” making the final choice harder.

Design response Move users into a personalized comparison view earlier.
05 — Iteration

The biggest changes were about completeness, freshness, and decision clarity.

The concept moved beyond “better recommendations” toward a system that explains what is current, what is missing, and why one option may fit better than another.

06 — Reflection

The project changed once the problem got bigger than the screen.

This project started as a better Zillow comparison table. Renter feedback reframed it. The harder problem was the work happening around the listing: checking other apps, verifying whether a floor plan is still available, reading reviews, checking commute, and then trying to remember why one “good” option was better than another.

The design opportunity became clearer: a better rental product should not just surface more homes. It should help renters trust what is current, see the information they actually need, and make a decision before search turns into overload.
