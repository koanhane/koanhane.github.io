01 · Origin

From story to game.

Veylora University · fictional institutional identity
The original written version was a UC Berkeley–based rules horror story. For the interactive adaptation, I rebuilt the setting as Veylora University — a fictional institution with its own identity, rules, and world.
How could I preserve the feeling of discovering contradictions while giving the player more agency than a written story?
Read original story ↗
02 · Design Challenge

Design challenge

In the original story, I controlled when the reader saw each rule and contradiction. In a game, the player needed to discover, remember, and connect information for themselves.

Discover

How can information feel found, rather than simply delivered by the story?

Explore

How can physical places like the library, dorm, and student union still matter?

Connect

How can scattered clues form one coherent investigation instead of unrelated strange events?

What I learned

  • The storyline felt fragmented.
  • Too many events appeared without one clear investigation thread.
  • Text was too small for a reading-heavy experience.
  • Dialogue progressed too quickly.
  • Some spaces reused visuals even after the player changed locations.
V2
V2 · One clear storyline

V2 shifted the focus toward one complete, coherent storyline. The library became the center of the investigation: earlier rules and events guided players toward it, discoveries there connected later scenes, and the full route eventually branched into a true ending and a false ending.

Core Mechanics

Awareness × Stability

V3 now tracks two hidden variables in the game state. Together, they create the tension between seeing the truth and succeeding inside the institution.

Awareness

Stability

How “normal” the university system believes the player is.

05 · User Testing

Formative test

I tested an incomplete V3 build to understand whether the new investigation direction was working before the full game was finished. The goal was not to validate the final experience, but to identify where players felt engaged, confused, or overly guided.
Prototype stage V3 · In progress
Format Moderated playtest
Main focus Discovery · Reading · Navigation
“I felt like I was being guided to look, rather than discovering things myself.”
01

The mystery worked, but discovery felt too guided.

The player understood the hidden fifth floor as the first major anomaly and could follow the library-centered investigation. However, many discoveries still felt prompted rather than self-directed.

Design response Reduce explicit prompts and give players more control over where to investigate next.
02

Reading friction reduced engagement.

Long blocks of text and weak hierarchy made some documents hard to scan. The player explicitly noted that dense text made them less willing to read.

Design response Shorter chunks, clearer hierarchy, more lists, and easier reopening of evidence.
03

The desktop system was one of the strongest parts.

The computer interface, archive, browser, and map were perceived as clear and visually coherent. This supported the direction of using Veylora OS as the investigation hub.

Design response Keep the OS central and make previously collected information easier to access at any time.
04

Early immersion was weaker than the later mystery.

The player understood the tasks, but did not immediately feel strongly embedded in the role of a new student. The strongest sense of unease began around the library sequence.

Design response Strengthen student identity, campus-life interactions, and onboarding before the horror becomes explicit.
Other issues observed Location changes felt abrupt, Student ID could be ignored without prompting, and some information architecture still needs simplification.
Prototype limitation A blocking bug prevented full validation of one ending path, so the ending flow still needs another test after implementation is complete.
Key takeaway

V3 should not simply tell players where the mystery is. It should give them better tools to notice, remember, and investigate it themselves.

Foundations

Visual language

The UI uses a restrained institutional palette: deep green for authority, cream for readability, and warm orange for emphasis. Editorial serif headings are paired with clean system sans-serif text so the experience feels more like a university ecosystem than a conventional horror interface.

Primary green Institutional surfaces, headers, buttons
Soft cream Backgrounds, readability, paper tone
Warm orange Accent, progress, subtle emphasis
Text ink Body copy, system labels, hierarchy

07 · Next Step

Next steps

After this formative test, the next step is not more abstract planning — it is implementation and refinement inside the game itself. I need to strengthen early immersion, reduce over-guidance, improve text hierarchy, add a quick way to reopen the OS and saved evidence, finish more V3 scenes and transitions, and then run another test once the blocked ending path is fully working.
ImmersionMake the player feel like a real new student earlier.
ExplorationShift from prompted actions to self-directed discovery.
ReadabilityBreak long text into clearer, scannable evidence formats.
RetestValidate the ending flow after implementation and bug fixes.
Reflection

From events to investigation.

The biggest shift was realizing that Veylora did not need more obvious horror. It needed better conditions for players to notice, remember, compare, and question information on their own.
